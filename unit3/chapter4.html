Fashion Brands

Chapter 4 • National & International Fashion Brands (Detailed)

Fashion brands are companies that create, market, and sell clothing, accessories, and related products under a specific name or identity. They range from small local labels to global luxury houses. Brands are typically categorized as national (strong presence in one country) or international (global reach across multiple countries and continents).

Classification of Fashion Brands

By Market Level / Positioning

Category Price Range Characteristics Examples
Haute Couture Extremely High Handmade, made-to-measure, exclusive Chanel, Dior, Valentino
Luxury / Designer Very High High-quality materials, brand prestige Louis Vuitton, Gucci, Prada
Premium / Bridge High–Medium Accessible luxury or high-quality ready-to-wear Michael Kors, Coach, Tory Burch
Contemporary Medium Trendy, good quality, mid-price Reformation, Everlane, Mango
Fast Fashion Low Quick trend replication, affordable, high volume Zara, H&M, Shein, Uniqlo
Mass Market / Value Very Low Basic, functional, budget-friendly Walmart, Primark, Old Navy

International Fashion Brands

Luxury & Designer Brands

  • Chanel (France) – Founded 1910 by Coco Chanel. Iconic for the little black dress, quilted handbag, and No.5 perfume. Known for timeless elegance.
  • Louis Vuitton (France) – Founded 1854. Famous for monogram canvas and luxury travel goods. Part of LVMH group.
  • Gucci (Italy) – Founded 1921. Known for bold designs, red-green stripe, and recent streetwear-luxury fusion under Alessandro Michele and Sabato De Sarno.
  • Dior (France) – Founded 1947 by Christian Dior. Famous for “New Look” silhouette and haute couture heritage.
  • Prada (Italy) – Founded 1913. Known for minimalist luxury, nylon backpacks, and intellectual aesthetic.
  • Balenciaga (Spain/France) – Founded 1917. Currently known for oversized silhouettes and controversial marketing.
  • Hermès (France) – Founded 1837. Famous for Birkin and Kelly bags, silk scarves, and extreme exclusivity.
  • Valentino (Italy) – Founded 1960. Renowned for red carpet gowns and “Valentino Red”.

Fast Fashion & Contemporary International Brands

  • Zara (Spain) – Founded 1975. Part of Inditex. Famous for fast-fashion model with 2–3 week design-to-store cycle.
  • H&M (Sweden) – Founded 1947. Global fast-fashion leader with many sub-brands (COS, & Other Stories, Monki).
  • Shein (China) – Ultra-fast fashion, data-driven, extremely short lead times, strong social media presence.
  • Uniqlo (Japan) – Founded 1949. Known for high-quality basics, innovative fabrics (Heattech, AIRism), and affordable prices.
  • Mango (Spain) – Contemporary fashion with strong European presence and growing global reach.
  • Forever 21 (USA) – Fast fashion targeting young consumers.
  • Primark (Ireland/UK) – Value fashion with very low prices and large stores.

National Fashion Brands (Country-Specific Examples)

India

  • Manyavar – Leading ethnic wear brand for men (sherwanis, kurtas).
  • Biba – Popular women’s ethnic wear brand.
  • Raymond – Iconic menswear and suiting brand since 1925.
  • Levi’s India – Strong national presence but international origin.
  • Reliance Trends / Shoppers Stop – Major retail chains with own labels.
  • Anita Dongre, Sabyasachi, Manish Malhotra – Prominent designer labels with strong domestic following.

USA

  • Ralph Lauren – Preppy American sportswear and luxury.
  • Tommy Hilfiger – Classic American casual wear.
  • Levi Strauss & Co. – Iconic denim brand (founded 1853).
  • Gap, Old Navy, Banana Republic – American casual/family brands.
  • Nike & Adidas – Global sportswear but strong American identity.
  • American Eagle, Hollister – Youth casual.

Europe (Selected National Brands)

  • United Kingdom: Burberry (heritage trench coats), ASOS (online), Ted Baker
  • France: Lacoste, Sandro, Maje, Claudie Pierlot
  • Italy: Armani, Versace, Dolce & Gabbana, Max Mara
  • Germany: Hugo Boss, Adidas (sport), Puma
  • Spain: Zara, Mango, Massimo Dutti (Inditex group)

Asia (Selected National Brands)

  • Japan: Uniqlo, Muji, Comme des Garçons, Issey Miyake
  • South Korea: Stylenanda, 8seconds, basic house brands
  • China: Li-Ning (sport), Bosideng (down jackets), Shein (global but Chinese origin)

Key Differences: National vs International Brands

Aspect National Brands International Brands
Market Reach Primarily one country or region Multiple countries and continents
Brand Identity Strong local cultural connection Global, often Western-centric image
Production & Supply Often locally sourced or smaller scale Global supply chains, large-scale production
Price Positioning Usually more affordable or niche Wide range from luxury to fast fashion
Marketing Local media, cultural relevance Global campaigns, celebrity endorsements
Examples Manyavar (India), Raymond (India) Zara, Gucci, Nike

Brand Growth Strategies

  • Expansion – Moving from national to international markets
  • Licensing & Franchising – Allowing others to use the brand name
  • Diffusion Lines – Lower-priced lines to reach broader audiences (e.g., DKNY, Armani Exchange)
  • Collaborations – H&M x Designer, Supreme x Louis Vuitton
  • Digital & Direct-to-Consumer – Growing through e-commerce and social media
